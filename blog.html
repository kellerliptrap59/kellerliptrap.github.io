How Have the Cleveland Cavaliers Performed in Their Last 10 Home Openers?

Analyzing Cleveland's home opener results, scoring, shooting efficiency, team statistics, and overtime games across 10 seasons.

The question

How have the Cleveland Cavaliers performed in their home openers over the last 10 seasons? I wanted to examine their win-loss record alongside points, shooting percentages, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers, and total player-minutes to identify potential overtime games.

The approach

Using SQL, I analyzed the Cavaliers' home opener game data across 10 seasons. I reviewed individual game results and compared them with average statistics to identify patterns in scoring, shooting efficiency, and overall team performance.

I also analyzed total team player-minutes. In a standard NBA regulation game, five players playing 48 minutes each results in 240 combined player-minutes (48 × 5). A total above 240 can indicate overtime because players accumulate additional minutes during extra periods. This provided another way to identify games that lasted beyond regulation. 

with cavs_games as (
select *,
ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
            PARTITION BY SEASON
            ORDER BY GAME_DATE ASC
        ) AS home_game_number
from cavs_last_10_seasons_box_scores
where SEASON_TYPE <> 'Playoffs' and MATCHUP like '% vs. %'
)
select GAME_DATE,MATCHUP, PTS, FG_PCT, FG3_PCT, REB, AST, STL, BLK, TOV, WL, PLUS_MINUS , MIN
from cavs_games
where home_game_number = 1
order by GAME_DATE

The results

The Cavaliers won 7 of their 10 home openers, giving them a 70% winning rate in the games analyzed. They averaged 112.6 points per game, with a 47.4% field goal percentage and a 34.2% three-point percentage.

Which NBA players ranked in the top 30 for points, rebounds, and assists?

Identifying players who ranked in the top 30 across three major statistical categories during the 2025–2026 NBA regular season.

The question

Which NBA players ranked in the top 30 in total points, assists, and rebounds during the 2025–2026 regular season?

The approach

Using pandas, I filtered the data to include regular-season games and grouped the player game logs by player name. I then summed points, assists, and rebounds to calculate each player's season totals across all games in the dataset.

Next, I used pandas' rank() method with method="dense" to rank players in each statistical category. Dense ranking assigns the same rank to tied values without skipping the next rank. Finally, I filtered the results to include only players ranked in the top 30 for all three categories simultaneously. 

games = pd.read_csv("nba_player_game_logs_2025_26.csv")

player_totals = (
    games[games["SEASON_TYPE"] == "Regular Season"]
    .groupby("PLAYER_NAME")[["PTS", "AST", "REB"]]
    .sum(numeric_only=True)
    .reset_index()
)
player_totals["points_rank"] = player_totals["PTS"].rank(method="dense", ascending=False)
player_totals["assists_rank"] = player_totals["AST"].rank(method="dense", ascending=False)
player_totals["rebounds_rank"] = player_totals["REB"].rank(method="dense", ascending=False)

print("Player where they rank in top 30 in points, assists, and rebounds:")
print(player_totals[(player_totals["points_rank"] <= 30) & (player_totals["assists_rank"] <= 30) & (player_totals["rebounds_rank"] <= 30)]))

The results

Six players ranked in the top 30 across all three categories: points, assists, and rebounds. Nikola Jokić and Jalen Johnson stood out in assists and rebounds, while Luka Dončić ranked first in total points and sixth in assists.

Which NBA Teams Outperformed League-Wide Averages in Five Key Statistics?

Comparing team averages for points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks during the 2025–2026 NBA season.

The question

Which NBA teams averaged above the league-wide averages in points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks during the 2025–2026 season?

The approach

Using pandas, I grouped the game-level data by team and calculated each team's average across five statistical categories. I then calculated the league-wide averages for the same categories and compared each team's performance against those benchmarks.

Finally, I filtered the results to identify teams that exceeded the league-wide average in all five categories simultaneously. This approach allowed me to compare team performance using consistent statistical benchmarks. 


df = pd.read_csv("nba_2025_2026_games.csv")

stats = ["PTS", "REB", "AST", "STL", "BLK"]

team_averages = (
    df.groupby("TEAM_NAME")[stats]
    .mean(numeric_only=True)
    .round(2)
    .reset_index()
)

nba_averages = (
    df[stats]
    .mean(numeric_only=True)
    .round(2)
)

above_avg_teams = team_averages[
    (team_averages["PTS"] > nba_averages["PTS"]) &
    (team_averages["REB"] > nba_averages["REB"]) &
    (team_averages["AST"] > nba_averages["AST"]) &
    (team_averages["STL"] > nba_averages["STL"]) &
    (team_averages["BLK"] > nba_averages["BLK"])
]

print(above_avg_teams)
print("League-wide averages:")
print(nba_averages)

The results

Only two teams exceeded the league-wide averages in all five categories: the Cleveland Cavaliers and Detroit Pistons. Both teams averaged more points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks than the league-wide benchmarks.

Did the Cleveland Cavaliers Play Worse on Back-to-Backs?

Comparing 2025–2026 results by days of rest. Little evidence of a fatigue effect, though there were only 14 back-to-backs.

The question

Did days of rest affect how the Cleveland Cavaliers played in the 2025–2026 regular season? Specifically, how do their average points, rebounds, steals, turnovers, effective field goal percentage (eFG%), point margin, and win-loss record on the second night of a back-to-back compare with games played on one day, two to three days, or four or more days of rest?

The approach

First, I built a temporary table of the Cavaliers' 82 regular-season games, ordered by date, and used the LAG() window function to calculate the days off since their previous game. I ordered by GAME_DATE rather than GAME_ID because the IDs for special games (opening night, Christmas, NBA Cup) are out of sequence. Games were also labeled home or away from the matchup text.

Next, I used a CTE to sort each game into a rest bucket: back-to-back (0 days off), 1 day, 2–3 days, or 4+ days. The 4+ bucket captures longer breaks like the All-Star break. I joined the buckets to the game box scores and grouped by bucket. eFG% is pooled, meaning total makes over total attempts, so games with more shots count for more. I used point margin (PLUS_MINUS) as the main outcome because it is more stable than win/loss. 

CREATE temporary table CAVS_GAMES AS(
select GAME_ID , GAME_DATE , TEAM_NAME, MATCHUP,
CASE
	WHEN MATCHUP LIKE '% vs. %' THEN 'HOME'
	ELSE 'AWAY'
    END AS LOCATION,
datediff(GAME_DATE, LAG(GAME_DATE) OVER (ORDER BY GAME_DATE)) - 1 AS REST_DAYS
from  nba_2025_2026_games games
where TEAM_NAME = 'Cleveland Cavaliers'
GROUP BY GAME_ID,GAME_DATE, MATCHUP, TEAM_NAME, MATCHUP
order by GAME_DATE
);
with rest_days as(
SELECT GAME_ID, GAME_DATE, LOCATION,
CASE
           WHEN rest_days = 0 THEN 'B2B'
           WHEN rest_days = 1 THEN '1 day'
           WHEN rest_days BETWEEN 2 AND 3 THEN '2-3 days'
           WHEN rest_days >= 4 THEN '4+ days'
		end as rest
FROM CAVS_GAMES
)
SELECT
    rd.rest                                         AS rest_bucket,
    COUNT(*)                                        AS games,
    ROUND(AVG(g.PTS), 1)                            AS avg_pts,
	ROUND(AVG(g.REB), 1)							AS avg_reb,
    ROUND(AVG(g.STL), 1)							AS avg_stl,
    ROUND(AVG(g.PLUS_MINUS), 2)                     AS avg_plus_minus,
    ROUND(SUM(g.FGM + 0.5 * g.FG3M) / SUM(g.FGA), 3) AS efg_pct,
    ROUND(AVG(g.TOV), 1)                            AS avg_tov,
    SUM(g.WL = 'W')                                 AS wins,
    SUM(g.WL = 'L')                                 AS losses
FROM rest_days rd
JOIN nba_2025_2026_games g
    ON rd.GAME_ID = g.GAME_ID
WHERE g.TEAM_NAME = 'Cleveland Cavaliers'
GROUP BY rd.rest
ORDER BY rd.rest;

The result

The query returns five rows: the four rest buckets plus a NULL row, which is the season opener (no prior game, so no rest value). The 82 games split into 14 back-to-backs, 54 games on one day of rest, 10 on two to three days, 3 on four or more, and the opener.

Takeaway: I found little evidence that the Cavaliers played worse on the second night of a back-to-back. Their average margin was +3.79 on back-to-backs versus +4.56 with one day of rest, a gap of less than one point. Their eFG% was close (.552 vs. .562), and they actually committed fewer turnovers on back-to-backs (12.2 per game vs. 13.8). The clearest gap is in record: 8-6 (57%) on back-to-backs versus 35-19 (65%) with one day of rest.

The 4+ day group had the highest margin (+7), but that is only three games, and the NULL row is the single season opener, so neither supports a conclusion.

Which Teams Did Donovan Mitchell Perform Best Against in the 2025–2026 NBA Regular Season?

His scoring was highest against Indiana, Orlando, and Atlanta, though most opponents have only two to four games.

The question

How did Donovan Mitchell's scoring, three-point shooting, field-goal shooting, and playing time vary against different opponents during the 2025–2026 NBA regular season?

The approach

I used a Common Table Expression (CTE) to isolate Donovan Mitchell's game-level statistics and identify each opponent from the matchup data. I joined the player box scores with the game records using the game ID and team abbreviation to match each performance to the correct game.

I then used aggregate functions, including AVG() and COUNT(), with GROUP BY to calculate his average performance and number of games against each opponent. Finally, I used ORDER BY to rank opponents by his average points per game. 

WITH donovan_mitchell_stats AS (
    SELECT 
        CONCAT(box.firstName, ' ', box.familyName) AS player_name,
        CASE
            WHEN game.MATCHUP LIKE 'CLE %' 
                THEN SUBSTRING_INDEX(game.MATCHUP, ' ', -1)
            ELSE SUBSTRING_INDEX(game.MATCHUP, ' ', 1)
        END AS opponent,
        game.GAME_DATE AS game_date,
        game.MATCHUP AS matchup,
        game.WL AS result,
        box.points AS pts,
        box.threePointersMade AS threes_made,
        box.threePointersPercentage AS three_pct,
        box.fieldGoalsMade AS fgm,
        box.fieldGoalsPercentage AS fg_pct,
        box.minutes AS min
    FROM nba_2025_2026_games AS game
    LEFT JOIN cavaliers_2025_2026_game_by_game AS box
        ON box.gameId = game.game_Id
        AND box.teamTricode = game.TEAM_ABBREVIATION
        AND box.personId = 1628378
    WHERE game.TEAM_ABBREVIATION = 'CLE' AND box.minutes IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT 
    player_name,
    opponent,
    COUNT(pts) AS games_played,
    ROUND(AVG(pts), 2) AS avg_points,
    ROUND(AVG(threes_made), 2) AS avg_threes_made,
    ROUND(AVG(three_pct), 3) AS avg_three_pct,
    ROUND(AVG(fgm), 2) AS avg_fgm,
    ROUND(AVG(fg_pct), 3) AS avg_fg_pct,
    ROUND(AVG(min), 2) AS avg_minutes
FROM donovan_mitchell_stats
GROUP BY player_name, opponent
ORDER BY avg_points DESC;

How Do the Cleveland Cavaliers Perform in Home and Away Wins and Losses?

Average points, assists, rebounds, and three-point shooting in wins and losses, at home and on the road.

The question

How do the Cleveland Cavaliers' average points, assists, rebounds, and three-point shooting percentage differ between wins and losses at home and on the road during the 2025–2026 NBA season?

The approach

I used a Common Table Expression (CTE) to isolate Cleveland Cavaliers game records and classify each game as home or away based on the matchup. I then grouped the games by result and location, calculating the average points, assists, rebounds, and three-point shooting percentage for each combination.

I used aggregate functions with GROUP BY to compare performance across the four categories: home wins, home losses, away wins, and away losses. I also rounded the averages to two decimal places to make the results easier to interpret. 

WITH cavs_stats AS (
    SELECT
        team_name,
        matchup,
        TRIM(REGEXP_REPLACE(matchup, 'CLE|vs\.|@', '')) AS opponent,
        CASE
            WHEN matchup LIKE '% vs. %' THEN 'HOME'
            ELSE 'AWAY'
        END AS location,
        wl,
        pts,
        ast,
        reb,
        fg3_pct
    FROM nba_2025_2026_games
    WHERE team_name = 'Cleveland Cavaliers'
)
SELECT
    wl AS result,
    location,
    ROUND(AVG(pts), 2) AS avg_points,
    ROUND(AVG(ast), 2) AS avg_assists,
    ROUND(AVG(reb), 2) AS avg_rebounds,
    ROUND(AVG(fg3_pct), 2) AS avg_3pt_percent
FROM cavs_stats
GROUP BY wl, location
ORDER BY
    FIELD(location, 'HOME', 'AWAY'),
    FIELD(result, 'W', 'L');

The result

The query produces four rows summarizing the Cavaliers' average performance: home wins, home losses, away wins, and away losses. Each row includes points, assists, rebounds, and three-point shooting percentage, allowing direct comparisons between winning and losing performances at different locations.
